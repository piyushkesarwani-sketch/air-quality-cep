Community survey

The questionnaire contained 10 questions and received 35 responses. It was mainly planned for Ghatkopar, Vidyavihar and Kurla, while responses from other areas were retained as collected.

What did people report?

  • 17 respondents (48.6%) rated local air quality as moderate.
  • 13 respondents (37.1%) said they notice air pollution often; another 13 said sometimes.
  • 21 respondents identified peak traffic hours as an important period for noticing pollution.
  • 17 respondents selected construction activities as a major noticed source.
  • 15 respondents selected more trees and green spaces as an improvement measure.
  • Only 3 respondents reported checking AQI regularly.

How the study was done

01 • Open data

Measurements were obtained from OpenAQ for the Ghatkopar, Mumbai–BMC monitoring location.

02 • Preparation

Records were organized by pollutant, date, time, value and unit before trend analysis.

03 • Daily averages

Selected measurements were grouped by date to create daily trend graphs.

04 • Community survey

A Google Forms questionnaire collected 35 responses about local air-quality perception.

05 • Comparison

Community perceptions provide context that can be considered alongside measured environmental indicators.

06 • Interpretation

Results are treated as a short-term case study, not as a complete long-term trend.

Key findings

Environmental data

  • PM2.5 showed an overall downward movement with short-term fluctuations.
  • PM10 showed greater variation and a clear peak on 29 September.
  • NO₂ showed noticeable fluctuations, with a peak on 26 September.
  • O₃ remained comparatively stable across the study period.

Community perspective

  • Moderate air quality was the most common perception.
  • Traffic hours and construction were frequently noticed in the survey.
  • Regular AQI checking was uncommon among respondents.
  • More trees and green spaces was the most selected improvement measure.

Important interpretation

The project uses an 11-day environmental observation window. It is useful for showing short-term fluctuations, but it should not be described as a definitive long-term air-quality trend. Longer monthly, seasonal and annual datasets would be needed for stronger trend conclusions.
